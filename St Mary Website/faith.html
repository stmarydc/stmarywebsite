The Coptic Faith

What We Believe

Orthodox, apostolic, ancient

The Coptic Orthodox Church is the Church of Alexandria, founded by St. Mark the Apostle. “Copt” comes from the Greek word for Egyptian.

Together with the Armenian, Syriac, Ethiopian, Eritrean, and Indian (Malankara) Orthodox Churches, we belong to the family of Oriental Orthodox Churches, which share one faith and communion.

  • The Holy Trinity: one God in three Persons, Father, Son, and Holy Spirit.
  • The Incarnation: Jesus Christ is fully God and fully man, one Lord, as St. Cyril of Alexandria taught: “one nature of God the Word incarnate,” without mingling, confusion, or change.
  • Salvation: by God’s grace, received through faith, the sacraments, and a life of repentance and love.
  • Holy Scripture and Holy Tradition: the Bible, read within the living teaching of the Church, the Councils, and the Fathers.
  • The communion of saints: we honor St. Mary the Theotokos, the martyrs, and all the saints, and ask their prayers.
The Creed

The Nicene Creed

Formulated at the Councils of Nicaea (325), where St. Athanasius of Alexandria defended the faith, and Constantinople (381), the Creed is proclaimed in every Liturgy. It begins:

“We believe in one God, God the Father, the Pantocrator, Creator of heaven and earth, and of all things seen and unseen…”

The Holy Mysteries

The seven sacraments

Through the sacraments, the Holy Spirit gives the grace of God to the faithful at every stage of life.

Baptism

New birth by water and the Spirit: full immersion, three times, in the name of the Holy Trinity.

Chrismation

The seal of the gift of the Holy Spirit, given with the holy Myron immediately after Baptism.

Eucharist

Holy Communion: the true Body and Blood of Christ, the center of the Church’s life.

Repentance & Confession

Returning to God, confessing before a priest, and receiving absolution and guidance.

Unction of the Sick

Prayer and anointing with oil for the healing of body and soul.

Matrimony

The crowning of husband and wife, united by God in one life of love.

Priesthood

The ordination of bishops, priests, and deacons to serve the Church.

Preparing for a sacrament?

Baptisms, weddings, and confession are arranged with Fr. Domadious.

Fasting

A Church that fasts

The Coptic Church keeps more fasting days than almost any other, over two hundred in a year.

Fasting means abstaining from all food for a period of the day, and then eating a vegan diet with no meat, dairy, or eggs. It is always joined to prayer, repentance, and giving to the poor. Fasting rules are adapted for children, the sick, and others under the guidance of one’s father of confession.

When a fast is under way, you’ll see a banner at the top of every page of this site.

Keeping Time

The Coptic calendar

The Church counts its years Anno Martyrum (A.M.), the “Year of the Martyrs,” beginning in AD 284 to honor those who died under Emperor Diocletian.

The calendar descends from the ancient Egyptian one. It has twelve months of thirty days each, plus a short thirteenth month of five or six days. The new year, the Feast of the Nayrouz, falls on September 11 (or 12 in the year before a Gregorian leap year).

Feasts, fasts, and the daily readings (the Katameros) and saints (the Synaxarium) all follow this calendar. See today’s Coptic date on our home page.

The Agpeya

Praying the hours

The Agpeya (from the Coptic word for “hour”) is the Coptic book of hours. Each hour of prayer remembers a moment in the life of Christ, with psalms, a Gospel reading, and prayers.

  1. Prime
    ~6 AM

    First Hour

    The Resurrection of Christ, the true Light, at the start of the day.

  2. Terce
    ~9 AM

    Third Hour

    The descent of the Holy Spirit at Pentecost, and Christ’s trial before Pilate.

  3. Sext
    ~12 PM

    Sixth Hour

    The Crucifixion of our Lord.

  4. None
    ~3 PM

    Ninth Hour

    The death of Christ on the Cross for our salvation.

  5. Vespers
    ~5 PM

    Eleventh Hour

    The taking down of Christ’s body from the Cross.

  6. Compline
    ~6 PM

    Twelfth Hour

    The burial of Christ, and preparing our souls for sleep.

  7. Midnight

    Midnight Prayer

    Three watches awaiting the Second Coming of Christ, the Bridegroom.

  8. Veil

    Prayer of the Veil

    A prayer of self-examination, traditionally prayed by monastics.
